The Problem

For every shift, staffing companies must answer: How much do I pay the worker? Time & attendance data comes from three conflicting sources:

1

Customer Systems

Facility timesheets from clock hardware, paper sign-ins, QR codes. Customer-controlled, often arrives late.

2

Time Tracking

Worker-submitted times via mobile app. Self-reported, prone to rounding and errors.

3

Location Intelligence

GPS tracking data. The ground truth of where workers actually were. Passive and objective.

A worker claims 10 hours while the facility says 8—who's right? That's where GPS and message context come in. Read full problem context →

Requirements

Part 1: Build the Engine

Parse & normalize data Handle CSV, JSON, and plain text formats. Deal with messy real-world data.
Detect & reason through conflicts Compare sources, use GPS as ground truth, factor in message context.
Produce recommendation Hours, payout amount, confidence score, human-readable explanation, flags for review.

Part 2: Generate Test Data

The 4 starter shifts are just examples. Use an LLM to generate your own expanded test dataset (minimum 10 scenarios) in the same format as the starter shifts. Real-world closeout has dozens of edge cases — we want to see which ones you'd prioritize.

Edge Cases to Consider

As you build your engine, think about scenarios like:

Late arrivals, early departures
Equipment failures, clock malfunctions
Weather delays, site closures
Double bookings, split shifts
Injury claims, incident reports
Training time at different rates
Break disputes (paid vs unpaid)
Multi-site work, travel time

Questions?

If something is unclear, that's intentional — real-world data is messy and ambiguous. Make reasonable assumptions and document them in your writeup.
