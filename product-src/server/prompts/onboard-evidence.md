# Payroll onboarding evidence

Condensed from `answers-to-onboarding.md` §2 (Ashish’s workflow and interview answer distributions). Counts describe observed answers, not probabilities or requirements. Goals can be covered together or in any useful order. Just-in-time questions from later phases are deliberately excluded.

## calendar
Frequency: weekly 7 (3 modern / 4 traditional), same-day or instant 4 (3 / 1), every two weeks 2 (1 / 1). Hours due Monday 4 (all traditional); Payroll closes Tuesday/Wednesday 3. Working default: Weekly, Sunday period end, cutoffDays 1, Friday payday, deadlineDays 2. Confirm rather than silently assume. Same-day schedules require clarification because the current calendar only represents Weekly, Biweekly, Semi-monthly and Monthly. Write set_calendar and cover_topic.

## workerHours
Paper 4 (1 / 3), email or photos 2 (0 / 2), text/call 4 (2 / 2), agency app or portal 28 (23 / 5), only clock at client 8 (4 / 4). Email/photos/paper can be forwarded or dropped; sheets shared; apps connected or scheduled exports forwarded; text screenshots forwarded. Ask for access the same way the team gets the data. Do not ask the software brand unless it changes access. Derive expected arrival from the calendar rather than asking again. If skipped, Set 1 is absent: disclose paying from Set 2 plus location evidence at the first closeout. Write profile.workerHours and source set 1; never invent an inbox address or a completed connection.

## clientHours
VMS/client clock 10 (4 / 6), signed sheet/photo 8 (6 / 2), emailed spreadsheet/PDF/screenshot 5 (2 / 3), supervisor phone/email 4 (1 / 3), approve in agency app 10 (9 / 1), shared sheet 1, no approval 2. Timing: 1–2 days after close 5 (1 / 4), 1–3 days after work 3, several days to two weeks 2, only at invoicing weeks later 6 (5 / 1). Connect VMS/browser, forward email, share sheet. Supervisor outreach needs permission. Default expectation is the cutoff. If approval arrives only with invoicing, pay from worker time plus location and reconcile client approval afterward. Write profile.clientHours and source set 2, including how and lag.

## whoseHours
39 companies answered the relevant items (multiple answers possible): client-approved 23 (18 / 5), compare both 13 (7 / 6), varies by client 3, location evidence 5 (4 / 1), pay worker and bill client 2. Default pay-of-record is client-approved, BUT never pay a worker less than they reported without evidence and approval above the authority limit. Client numbers govern billing, not automatic permission to cut pay. A self-reported-hours legal obligation and major unpaid-break litigation appeared in the interviews. Write profile.whoseHours and a guardrail rule; record explicit per-client exceptions.

## rates
Contracts/rate cards 4 (0 / 4), internal SOP 11 (8 / 3), state law mainly 3 (0 / 3), in someone’s head 6 (6 / 0), nothing written 4 (3 / 1). Back-office rates are a sensible option without an interview count. Special-pay examples: meal/rest rules 4, holiday double time 1, overtime combined across clients 1; differentials are a catalog option, not counted. Firm pre-read supplies likely states; confirm uncertain facts. Drop/forward contracts, share rate sheets, connect back office. If skipped, federal/state catalog only and rates from the Payroll export. Law and contracts strict; SOP bendable pending later confirmation. Write profile.ratesWhere; add_rule records user-stated rules for review, never fabricated contract provisions.

## complaints
Where complaints arrive (33 respondents): client/site 12 (9 / 3), phone/walk-in 2 (0 / 2), found internally 2 (0 / 2), email 2, text 2, tickets/chat 6 (all modern), app 6 (5 / 1). Ownership (39 respondents): Payroll/operator 12 (3 / 9), ops/branch 13 (11 / 2), support 6 (all modern), account managers 4, billing/AR 3. CC/forward email, connect helpdesk, record phone/walk-in notes. Skipped default: signed-in operator owns complaints; the agent handles only messages it receives and discrepancies it finds. Write profile.complaints with channel, owner and hand-off.

## authority
Always ask first 7 (3 / 4); pay what reported 7 (5 / 2); only small gaps 3 (examples 10, 45, 60 minutes); only clean-record workers 2. Contact evidence: phone/in-person 4 (traditional), email 2 (traditional), text 3, ticket queue 3 (traditional), account managers 4 (modern). Existing suggested settings are autoFix true, $100 per entry, $1,000 weekly, supervisor texts yes, worker texts no, Email briefing; these are proposals, not consent. Traditional operations often prefer ask-first. Ask for explicit limits/contact intent and persist the user’s explicit choices with set_authority, especially ask-first restrictions; unconfirmed suggestions remain proposals for the editable Rulebook. Never activate autonomous action or outreach silently. Never use a de minimis tolerance to withhold California work time. Record never-contact names immediately. Write set_authority for explicit choices, profile.notes for nuance and unconfirmed proposals, and cover_topic. Rulebook is the manual authority editor.
