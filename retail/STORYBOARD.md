# /retail — "Accurate billing is a talent strategy" storyboard

The retail-buyer cut of the /story deck (Kashyap's script, Sep 19 2026): seven slides for a retailer's procurement, finance and accounts payable leaders who buy contingent labor through agencies and a VMS. Same illustration language, deck.css and deck.js as `../story/` — read `../story/CLAUDE.md` first; every rule there applies. Every frame's prompt is the STYLE block verbatim, a blank line, then the SCENE verbatim.

## STYLE (prefix for every frame — do not vary)

Storyboard panel, 16:9 widescreen, detailed editorial graphic-novel illustration. Confident black ink linework with flat cel shading and a subtle paper grain. Strictly limited palette: deep charcoal (#141416) shadows, warm cream (#efe9dc) paper lights, cool slate mid-tones, and exactly ONE signal colour, HyperTrack green (#22c55e), used sparingly on the single most important element in the frame. Cinematic composition with clear foreground / mid-ground / background depth, one dramatic directional light source, realistic adult human proportions, contemporary American logistics, hospitality and healthcare workplaces, lots of believable environmental detail. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, logos, readable UI copy or watermarks anywhere in the image. Not 3D render, not clay, not chibi, not cartoon mascot, not photoreal.

## Frames

### 01 · why-were-here — "We're here to lower what you pay for contingent labor."
Interior, the glass-walled operations office of a national retailer's distribution center at peak season, late afternoon. Camera behind and to the left of a procurement director in a dark blazer, hand at her chin, studying a wide monitor. The screen shows an abstract vendor list: six rows, each with a small pictogram of a different staffing agency and two plain bars of different lengths, no words; the top row is ringed in green with a green dot, the single row that checks out. Through the window beyond her, the distribution floor at full peak: conveyors of cartons, pallets stacked to the rafters, three agency crews in three different styles of hi-vis vest, a forklift, trailers backed to every dock door. On the desk: a mug, a pen pot, a printed invoice, a plant. The single idea: many agencies, many hours, one person trying to check them at peak. Only the ringed row and its dot are green.

### 02 · moment-of-truth — "The shift doesn't end at clock-out. It ends when she's paid."
Dusk at the open dock door of a retail fulfilment center in the holiday rush. Foreground, large in frame: a woman picker in her thirties in a hi-vis vest over a hoodie, sitting on the dock edge at the end of her shift, tired and content, looking down at her phone. Above the phone float two abstract message bubbles with no words, the second one green, and beside them a translucent pay-stub panel drawn as three plain rows with a green check on the middle row. Behind her inside the building: roll cages of parcels, gift-wrapped cartons on a conveyor, a co-worker with a pallet jack, a forklift. Outside: a trailer being loaded, a light tower, a long sunset sky in cream and slate. A steel travel mug beside her. The single idea: for her the shift ends when the pay lands. Only the second bubble and the check are green.

### 03 · bill-and-pay — "Their middle office, your accounts payable — the promise breaks in between."
Night, a straight-on cut-away of two office buildings side by side with a narrow alley between them, city lights behind. On the left, a staffing agency's middle office: five tired people at desks buried in paper timesheets, spreadsheets on old monitors, a coffee pot, a wall clock, one person head-down on folded arms. On the right, a retailer's accounts payable floor: four clerks with towers of agency invoices, rubber stamps, calculators, an approval tray overflowing. Between the buildings, across the alley at desk height, a single sagging rope bridge of clipped-together paper, sheets slipping off it and falling into the dark. From the roofline of both buildings one thin green line descends toward the gap, about to tie the two floors together. The single idea: the promise breaks in the gap between their office and yours. Only the descending line is green.

### 05 · closes-in-your-vms — "Then it closes out in your VMS. Agentically."
Interior, a retailer's accounts payable office at dawn, a window behind looking onto a store delivery yard with box trucks. At a desk sits a translucent green wireframe figure, the digital worker, typing at a keyboard. In front of it floats a large holographic browser window showing a blank vendor-management grid, rows and columns with no words and one solid green button. A small conveyor runs across the desk carrying agency invoice cards out of a letter tray and up into a slot at the base of the window; each card that has passed shows a small green dot, and two cards wait to one side in a wire tray with a paper-clipped evidence sheet on each. A stopwatch on the desk shows a thin green wedge. An empty office chair with a jacket, a desk lamp, pens, filing cabinets, a plant. The lower centre of the frame is calm, dark desk surface. The single idea: verified invoices post themselves into the VMS; disputed ones wait with their evidence. Only the wireframe worker, the window glow, the button, the dots and the wedge are green.

### 06 · comes-back — "Pay him right and he comes back to your building."
Dawn, wet tarmac outside a big-box retailer's regional distribution center, mist and low sun. Foreground left, large in frame, seen from behind his shoulder: a warehouse associate in his thirties, cap, beard, hi-vis vest over a work jacket, a paper coffee cup in one hand and his phone in the other, the screen showing a small truck pictogram above a green ring with a check, no words. From the phone a dotted green line runs across the yard to one dock door, which is outlined in green with a geofence ring and tick marks. In the yard: unmarked tractor-trailers, a forklift carrying shrink-wrapped pallets, two co-workers in vests walking in, yellow bollards, puddles catching the light. The single idea: he picked this building again, and he is walking back in. Only the phone screen, the dotted line and the dock-door ring are green.

### 07 · one-coin — "Accurate billing and reliable service are two sides of the same coin."
Low angle across a polished boardroom table at night, a retailer's distribution center lit up through the window behind, trailers at the docks. In the centre a large coin spins on its edge, motion arcs around it, its rim glowing green. One face, turned toward the viewer, is engraved with a paid invoice: a sheet with plain line rows and a seal with a check. The other face, caught in the spin and partly visible, is engraved with a store associate in a vest beside a loaded roll cage. From the left a work-worn hand in a hi-vis cuff reaches toward the coin; from the right a hand in a suit cuff with a cufflink reaches toward it. Around the table: a steel mug, a glass of water, a pen, a closed laptop, empty chairs. The single idea: the invoice and the worker are the same coin. Only the coin's rim is green.
