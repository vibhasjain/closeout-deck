# Middle office storyboard — "Protect your revenue"

Twelve frames, one illustration language. Every image prompt starts with the STYLE block verbatim, then the SCENE.

## STYLE (prefix for every frame — do not vary)

Storyboard panel, 16:9 widescreen, detailed editorial graphic-novel illustration. Confident black ink linework with flat cel shading and a subtle paper grain. Strictly limited palette: deep charcoal (#141416) shadows, warm cream (#efe9dc) paper lights, cool slate mid-tones, and exactly ONE signal colour, HyperTrack green (#22c55e), used sparingly on the single most important element in the frame. Cinematic composition with clear foreground / mid-ground / background depth, one dramatic directional light source, realistic adult human proportions, contemporary American logistics, hospitality and healthcare workplaces, lots of believable environmental detail. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, logos, readable UI copy or watermarks anywhere in the image. Not 3D render, not clay, not chibi, not cartoon mascot, not photoreal.

## Frames

### 01 · the-lunch — "You're about to get your lunch eaten."
Night. Interior of a traditional staffing agency branch office, cluttered and tired: grey filing cabinets, paper timesheets pinned to a corkboard, a fax machine, a desk phone with a blinking light, one recruiter in a rumpled shirt under buzzing fluorescent tubes, a half-eaten sandwich in paper wrap beside a stack of pay-stub envelopes. Through the wide window across the street: a sleek glass headquarters of a tech-enabled staffing startup, every floor lit, silhouettes at standing desks, screens glowing green, the only green in the frame. The startup's glow throws a long shadow across the recruiter's desk and the sandwich. Low camera, slight Dutch tilt.

### 02 · the-gap — "They will build and deploy AI so much faster than you."
Dusk, low drone angle over a two-lane highway that runs to the horizon. Left lane: a boxy vintage delivery truck overloaded with cardboard boxes of paper files, doors flapping, three workers in polo shirts pushing it from behind. Right lane, far ahead and blurred with speed: a streamlined electric hauler with green tail-lights, rear doors open showing neat racks of servers. Between them a long stretch of empty dashed lane markers, the gap itself is the subject. Highway signs are blank. Long shadows, dust.

### 03 · already-matched — "You've already applied AI at recruiting and matching."
Sunrise at a distribution centre loading dock, morning fog, semi-trailers backed into bays, pallets, a forklift. Foreground: a warehouse worker in a hi-vis vest leaning on a bollard, coffee in one hand, phone in the other. The phone screen glows green with an abstract job card and a confidence dial drawn as a filled arc, no words. Thin dotted lines drawn like a technical overlay connect the phone to a specific bay door across the lot, the match happening in real time. Confident, calm mood.

### 04 · middle-office — "Next, AI percolates into the middle office."
Cut-away dollhouse cross-section of a three-storey staffing company office, seen straight on like an architectural section, interior fully detailed. Top floor: recruiting and matching, bright, modern, curved monitors with soft green glow, people relaxed. Ground floor: sales and reception, plants, a couch. Middle floor, the heart of the frame: the middle office, rows of desks buried under paper timesheets, calculators, three monitors per desk each showing a different conflicting spreadsheet grid, a wall clock at eleven, a burnt coffee pot, one person asleep on folded arms. A single thread of green light is just beginning to trickle down the stairwell from the top floor into the middle floor. Night outside the windows.

### 05 · trust — "When pay and bill work, workers trust the platform."
Friday evening city bus interior, warm interior light, rain streaks on the windows, city lights outside. Foreground: a home-care nurse in scrubs, tired but relaxed, holding her phone: the screen shows a large green check mark and a deposit tile, abstract, no words. Beside her a tote with a child's crayon drawing sticking out, a folded grocery list, a lunch container. Other passengers dozing. Her face says relief: it arrived, it was fast, and it was right.

### 06 · revolving-door — "Workers churn when you pay them wrong."
Rainy night on a sidewalk outside a staffing branch with a big brass revolving door. A continuous stream of workers with duffel bags and lunch coolers enters one side and exits the other, a few tearing up paper pay stubs, a few looking at their phones with disappointment. Inside, through the glass, a scheduler with a phone pressed to one ear stares at a wall-mounted shift board full of empty blank rectangles. Green is almost absent from this frame, only a flickering green exit sign above the door. Wet reflections on the pavement.

### 07 · the-choice — "Every day, workers choose between your platform and two others."
Five in the morning, small kitchen, single warm lamp. A warehouse worker in a hoodie sits at the table with coffee, work boots by the door, a packed lunchbox, a toddler asleep on the couch in the background under a blanket. The hero of the frame is the phone propped against the sugar jar: the home screen shows three identical-sized abstract app icons in a row, and the worker's thumb hovers between them. Only one of the three icons glows green. The whole marketplace is that phone.

### 08 · left-hanging — "Worse yet, they will leave you hanging at the last minute."
Shift start at a warehouse pick line, harsh overhead light. A line supervisor with a clipboard checks a wristwatch; along the line one station is empty: a folded hi-vis vest on the stool, an idle scanner, an empty tote. The rest of the crew glance sideways at the gap. Through the open roll-up door, the shuttle van has already pulled away, tail-lights in the dark. The empty station's terminal screen blinks green, the only green in the frame.

### 09 · vms-choice — "Every day, your customers choose on the VMS."
Daylight, glass-walled facility office overlooking a distribution floor. A procurement manager sits at a large monitor showing an abstract vendor management system: a column of supplier cards, each with a fill-rate bar and a cost bar, no words. One new card at the top, minimal and clean, is highlighted green; older cluttered supplier cards are sliding down the ranking. On the desk, a printed contract with a pen, a mug, a paper cup of pens. Through the glass, workers in vests on the floor below.

### 10 · flywheel — "If you don't pay as excellently as the new guys, the flywheel reverses."
Interior of a vast turbine hall, dramatic top light, dust in the beams. A giant industrial flywheel fills the frame. On the upper rim, tiny figures of workers with duffel bags and hard hats walk along the wheel; on the lower rim, tiny figures of customers in office wear with clipboards. In the foreground a staffing-agency owner in rolled sleeves leans their whole body weight against a spoke, straining, but the motion lines show the wheel has begun to turn backwards. A thin green direction indicator glows on the rim, the only green.

### 11 · one-coin — "Revenue protection and worker retention are two sides of the same coin."
Boardroom table at night, single hard spotlight. A large coin stands on edge mid-spin, motion-blurred, caught in the light. One face is engraved with a worker's profile in a hard hat; the other with a customer's warehouse building and a rising bar chart. From opposite sides of the table two different hands reach toward it, one a worker's hand with a callused palm, the other a customer's hand in a suit cuff. Neither has it. The rim of the coin glints green.

### 12 · real-time — "Now we can notice exceptions and address them in real time."
Dawn at a distribution centre parking lot. A worker walks from her car across the asphalt toward the entrance; drawn on the ground around the building is a glowing green geofence ring, rendered as a precise technical overlay with tick marks. Her phone buzzes with an abstract SMS bubble and a check mark. Hovering above her, a semi-transparent isometric holographic panel drawn in green wireframe shows a shift timeline with one exception being resolved into a clean bar. In the far background the middle-office windows are dark, nobody stayed late. Optimistic mood.

## Act 3 — Payroll ops, the AI story

### 13 · two-questions — "Every shift has to answer two questions."
Night, a payroll clerk's workbench seen from directly above, one desk lamp. In the centre a single completed-shift card, blank and abstract, with a worker's badge photo silhouette. To its left a worker's pay envelope with a cheque peeking out; to its right a customer invoice on a clipboard with a stamp pad. The clerk's two hands hold two different pens, one hovering over each. Around the edges: a calculator, a stapler, coffee ring stains, a stack of other shift cards waiting. The shift card in the centre glows faintly green.

### 14 · three-clocks — "The answer comes from three sources that disagree."
One frame split into three vertical panels like a comic strip. Left panel: a wall-mounted badge kiosk at a warehouse entrance with a paper sign-in clipboard hanging beside it and a round wall clock above. Middle panel: a worker's hand holding a phone with an abstract clock-in app, a second round clock on the screen. Right panel: a bird's-eye map drawn as a technical overlay, a dotted GPS trail from a car park to a building outline, a third clock drawn in the corner. All three clock faces show visibly different times (hands only, no numerals). Only the GPS trail is green.

### 15 · rulebook-in-heads — "The rulebook lives in people's heads."
A payroll clerk at a desk drowning in institutional memory: a fat three-ring binder of client contracts propped open, a union agreement booklet, dozens of sticky notes on the monitor bezel and wall, a hand-drawn cheat sheet taped to the desk, a rolodex, a desk phone ringing with its light blinking, two mugs. The clerk has one finger on a binder clause and the other hand on a calculator, eyes closed, reciting from memory. Daylight through blinds. One single sticky note is green.

### 16 · compile — "Compile the rulebook once. Run it on every shift."
Interior of a clean modern print shop, cinematic side light. On the left, a stack of binders, contracts and handwritten rule sheets is fed into an elegant industrial machine drawn like a cross between a letterpress and a card-punch compiler, all brass rollers and glass panels. Out of the right side rolls a clean continuous strip of punched cards, each one a shift, moving along a conveyor under a stamping head. Gears mesh inside the glass. The stamp head glows green as it marks each card.

### 17 · agent-conversation — "The agent asks the worker, not the clerk."
A worker on break sitting on the edge of a loading dock at dusk, legs dangling, coffee beside her, phone in hand. Her phone shows an abstract text-message thread: one incoming bubble, one outgoing bubble, no words. Floating beside her, a semi-transparent holographic panel drawn in thin wireframe shows a shift timeline with a single flagged gap; a check mark is replacing the flag. In the background, forklifts and pallets, the warehouse humming. Only the check mark is green.

### 18 · human-queue — "Humans handle only what needs a human."
Bright morning, a calm operations desk by a window with plants. One ops person, relaxed, coffee in hand, looking at three shift cards laid neatly on the desk, that is the whole queue. Beside the desk a recycling bin overflowing with the paper timesheet mountain from before. On the wall a clock at nine in the morning. Through the window, the parking lot fills with workers arriving. The top card of the three glows green.

### 19 · receipt — "Every decision shows its work."
Close-up of a detective's evidence board, but for one payout. Pinned in the centre: an abstract pay stub. Connected to it by taut string and pushpins: a photo of a badge kiosk clock, a small map with a GPS trail, a highlighted clause torn from a contract page, a printed text-message bubble, a customer's signed approval slip. Warm lamp light from the side, corkboard texture, a magnifying glass resting on the ledge below. The string connecting the evidence is green.
